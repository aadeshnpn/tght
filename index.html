Along the Way

Join a stretch of the pilgrimage

MedOnMt’s Great Himalaya Trail journey is a fundraising pilgrimage for a free meditation sanctuary. Along the way, a few short sections will open to anyone who has the fitness, the schedule, a love of the mountains, and a real eagerness for this cause — and who wants to walk the path for themselves.

About 2–4 people can join each stretch, with roughly 20–30 across the whole pilgrimage, so the experience stays quiet and personal. One example: a few days through the Khumbu valley. You cover your own travel, permits, gear, food, and insurance. You simply join for a firsthand taste of the pilgrimage.

How to apply for those seats will be shared later. If that kind of walk calls to you, watch this space — or reach out at aadesh@medonmt.org.

The Reality Check

GHT High Route vs. Pacific Crest Trail (PCT)

Alpine Mountaineering vs. Graded Singletrack

While the Pacific Crest Trail traverses 2.5× the horizontal distance (4,265 km vs 1,700 km), the GHT demands comparable total vertical climb (+120,000m vs +128,000m) at more than double the vertical steepness (70.6 m/km vs 30.0 m/km). Furthermore, the GHT's average elevation (~4,200m) is higher than the PCT's highest mountain pass (Forester Pass, 4,009m)—requiring crampons, fixed ropes, and self-supported survival over 6,000m glaciated cols rather than graded equestrian trail.

The Atmospheric Law

The Continuous Hypoxia Crucible

On classic American thru-hikes, hikers occasionally touch 4,000m for an hour before dropping back to lush valleys. On the GHT High Route:

▶ 85+ days spent sleeping above 4,000m.
▶ Barometric pressure drops to ~475 hPa (less than half of sea level).
▶ Requires deep somatic breathing & mindful acclimatization pacing.

The Hidden Beyuls

In Tibetan Buddhism, Guru Padmasambhava consecrated sacred hidden valleys (*beyul*) throughout the Himalayas as sanctuaries of spiritual refuge. Sections of the GHT pass through these sanctified spaces where meditation practice is said to bear hundredfold fruit.

🏔️

The Bonpo of Dolpo

In Upper Dolpo, time has preserved Yungdrung Bon — the pre-Buddhist spiritual tradition of Tibet. Here, chortens are circled counter-clockwise, and ancient monasteries cling to cliffs beneath the sacred Crystal Mountain (*Shey Shelri*).

🌊

Sacred Alpine Lakes

High holy waters dot the route: Gosainkunda where Shiva plunged his trident to cool the poison of the cosmos, the unearthly turquoise jewel of Phoksundo Lake, and the tranquil alpine mirror of Rara.

Curiosity & Inquiry

Questions of the High Route

Everything you wanted to understand about traversing the highest corridor on Earth.

Physical fitness is only half the equation. Acclimatization requires gradual elevation gain, staying hydrated, and honoring rest days. Mentally, vipassana and mindfulness practice teach the hiker to observe physical exhaustion, blister pain, and cold without escalating them into psychological suffering. The mountain strips away everything non-essential.
Sherpani Col (6,150m), West Col (6,190m), and Amphu Labsta (5,845m) connect the Barun, Hongu, and Khumbu valleys. They represent the most technical and exposed mountaineering barrier on the trail, featuring fixed ropes, crevasse fields, and vertical rock and ice descents with zero evacuation options.
The GHT traverses multiple restricted border areas requiring special permits: Kanchenjunga Restricted Permit, Makalu Barun National Park, Sagarmatha National Park, Manaslu Restricted Area, Upper Mustang Restricted Permit, and Upper Dolpo. Total permit costs often exceed $2,500 per person and require registered guide logistics.
Mindfulness is not carelessness; it is razor-sharp situational awareness. Paying absolute attention to foot placement, weather changes, snowpack stability, and body signals is mindfulness in its highest practical expression. Our philosophy is that mountain safety begins with a quiet, observant mind.

Expedition Appendix & Research Bibliography

Every geospatial elevation profile, atmospheric simulation, crux pass assessment, and metabolic model across this project is grounded in authoritative field cartography, official Nepalese governmental charters, satellite digital elevation models (DEM), and peer-reviewed altitude medicine literature.

Trail Charters & Governance

Official Nepalese Authorities
Charter
  • The Great Himalaya Trail (Official Portal) greathimalayatrail.com ↗

    Foundational definition of the Nepal High Route (~1,700 km, max elevation 6,190m; official materials cite ~150,000m+ of ascent and descent). This site uses ~120,000m cumulative ascent for gain comparisons. Low Cultural Route (~1,500 km, max ~4,519m).

  • Official GHT Thru-Hiker Database (HAL & Robin Boustead) himalayanadventurelabs.com ↗

    Authoritative registry documenting all 133 verified human-powered 'Full Nepal GHT' crossings border-to-border, co-curated by Himalayan Adventure Labs and trail founder Robin Boustead.

  • Nepal Tourism Board (NTB) ntb.gov.np ↗

    National trekking regulatory authority, TIMS registration framework, mandatory guide mandates for foreign trekkers (2023+ policy), and sustainable Himalayan ecotourism guidelines.

  • Department of Immigration, Nepal nepalimmigration.gov.np ↗

    Restricted Area Permit (RAP) legal quotas, minimum party sizes, and fee schedules for Upper Mustang ($500/10 days), Upper Dolpo ($500/10 days), Manaslu, Kanchenjunga, and Nar-Phu frontier corridors.

  • SNV Netherlands Development Organisation snv.org ↗

    Pioneering GHT Sustainable Tourism Development Project (2008–2014) in partnership with NTB and DFID, establishing pro-poor, community-based micro-economies across high mountain valleys.

Field Cartography & DEM

Geospatial Datasets & Topography
Geospatial
  • Himalaya Map House (HMH) Kathmandu, Nepal

    Official 1:100,000 and 1:50,000 GHT Topographic Map Series (Series 100 to 109) surveyed by Robin Boustead. The definitive physical paper navigation benchmark for high passes, moraine trails, and water points.

  • OpenStreetMap (OSM) & OpenAndroMaps openstreetmap.org ↗

    International Walking Network (network=iwn, route=hiking) vector geometries powering our interactive Leaflet map and GeoJSON coordinates (data/ght-full-route.geojson).

  • NASA SRTM & ESA Copernicus 30m Global DEM WGS 84 / GLO-30

    Satellite radar and radar interferometry elevation rasters used to calibrate pass elevations, calculate slope gradients, and compute the ~120,000m cumulative vertical ascent (High Route) across the entire Nepal High Route.

  • FarOut Navigation (Guthook Guides) faroutguides.com ↗

    Thru-hiker waypoint log, glacial retreat bypass notes, high-camp coordinates, and seasonal water source dependability across remote non-teahouse sections (e.g. Makalu Three Cols, Teri La).

Atmospheric Physics & Physiology

Mathematical Models & Altitude Medicine
Bio-Physics
  • International Standard Atmosphere (ISA) Formula Barometric Equations

    Models barometric pressure $P/P_0 = (1 - L\cdot h / T_0)^{gM/R_0L}$ powering our live atmospheric simulator: deriving effective oxygen fractions (67% at 3,000m down to 47% at 6,190m West Col).

  • Pandolf & Givoni-Goldman Metabolic Load Formulation J. Appl. Physiol.

    Metabolic expenditure equation calibrated for heavy expedition packs ($L = 18\text{--}24\text{ kg}$) and alpine moraine/snow terrain coefficients ($\eta = 2.1\text{--}2.5$), projecting daily caloric burn between 4,800 and 6,200 kcal/day.

  • Wilderness Medical Society (WMS) & ISMM Guidelines ↗

    Clinical Practice Guidelines for Acute Altitude Illness: dictating acclimatization sleep ceilings (maximum $\le 500\text{m}$ net sleep elevation gain per 24h above 3,000m) and AMS/HAPE/HACE protocol thresholds.

  • Historic Traverses & FKT Benchmarks Athletic Records

    Ryan Sandes & Ryno Griesel (2018: 25d 4h 24m supported); Andrew Porter (2016: 28d 13h 36m solo unsupported); Asimina Inglezou (2024: 37 high route days, first solo woman).
